Sell My House in State College, PA: Pricing, Prep and a Clean Closing

Listing services from Barb Alpert, REALTOR®, Associate Broker — Kissinger Bigatel & Brower

Most homes that sit on the market in Centre County were not badly marketed. They were badly priced, or they went to market before they were ready. Both are fixable — before the listing goes live, not after.

What does the home selling process look like in Centre County?

  1. Walkthrough and honest assessment. Barb walks the house with you and tells you what buyers here will react to — good and bad.
  2. Pricing. A comparative market analysis built from real closed sales in your specific neighborhood and property type, not a national algorithm. Start with a free valuation.
  3. Preparation. A short, prioritized punch list: what genuinely returns more than it costs, and what to skip.
  4. Photography and marketing. Professional photos, accurate copy, MLS syndication and the reach of Kissinger Bigatel & Brower, one of the market-leading brokerages in State College.
  5. Showings and feedback. You’ll hear what buyers actually said, weekly, not a vague “lots of interest.”
  6. Offer review and negotiation. Net proceeds, financing strength, contingencies, timing — the highest number isn’t always the best offer.
  7. Inspection, appraisal, closing. The stage where deals fall apart. This is where an Associate Broker with 30+ years earns her keep.

How should I price my home in Happy Valley?

Pricing is not a guess and it is not aspiration. It’s a read on three things: what genuinely comparable homes closed for recently, what’s currently competing with you, and how much of the qualified buyer pool your price band actually reaches.

Overpricing costs more than time. The first two to three weeks bring the most motivated, best-prepared buyers in the market. If your price pushes them past you, you spend the next two months chasing the market down and eventually sell for less than a correct price would have brought — with a stale days-on-market number working against you.

What’s worth fixing before you list?

  • Almost always worth it: deep cleaning, decluttering, fresh neutral paint where it’s tired, landscaping cleanup, and anything that reads as deferred maintenance from the curb.
  • Often worth it: replacing failed or fogged windows, resolving a known roof or gutter issue, servicing the HVAC, addressing damp basement smells.
  • Usually not worth it: full kitchen or bath remodels immediately before listing. Buyers rarely pay back the whole cost, and they’d have picked different finishes.
  • For farms and acreage: fence repair, brush-hogging pasture, tidying the barn aisle and having well and septic records in hand. See our farms & equestrian page.

Seller FAQs

What are real estate commissions and how are they set?

Commissions are fully negotiable and are never set by law, by any association, or by the MLS. Whatever you and the brokerage agree to is spelled out in your written listing agreement before the home goes on the market, including any compensation offered to a buyer’s broker. Barb will walk you through the numbers and your net proceeds before you sign.

How long will it take to sell my house in State College?

It depends heavily on price band, condition and location, and it shifts through the year — the Penn State calendar genuinely moves this market. We’d rather give you current numbers for your specific street than post a figure here that goes stale. Call and ask.

Should I sell before I buy, or buy before I sell?

Both work; the right answer depends on your equity, your financing and your risk tolerance. We’ll map out the sequencing options — including post-settlement occupancy and contingent offers — and their real trade-offs. See the buyer side.

Do I have to disclose problems with my house?

Yes. Pennsylvania sellers must complete a Seller’s Property Disclosure Statement covering known material defects. Disclosing honestly is both the law and, practically speaking, the best way to keep a deal from unraveling at inspection. We’ll help you complete it thoroughly.

What’s different about selling a horse farm or property with acreage?

A different buyer pool, a longer marketing runway, and an appraisal that has to account for barns, fencing, arenas and land. It also needs someone who can speak credibly to horse people. That’s Barb’s specialty — see the farms & equestrian page.
