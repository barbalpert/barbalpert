Homes for Sale in State College, PA: Neighborhood Guide

From Barb Alpert, REALTOR®, Associate Broker — Kissinger Bigatel & Brower

State College is a borough of a few square miles wrapped in townships that most people also call State College. Where the borough ends and College Township begins matters enormously to your taxes, your walk to campus and what you’ll pay per square foot — and it’s almost invisible on a map app.

What is it like to live in State College?

Penn State sets the rhythm. The town swells in August, empties in May, and on seven or eight Saturdays each fall it becomes one of the larger population centers in Pennsylvania for an afternoon. Locals plan around it and mostly enjoy it — a small town with a Big Ten university’s worth of concerts, lectures, medical care and restaurants attached.

The tradeoff is a housing market shaped by an institution. Student rental demand props up values near campus, university hiring drives the professional buyer pool, and inventory in the most desirable neighborhoods is genuinely tight. Buyers who understand that arrive ready and do well.

What are the best neighborhoods in State College, PA?

“Best” depends entirely on what you’re optimizing for. Here’s the honest shape of each:

  • College Heights — the classic walk-to-campus neighborhood just north of Park Avenue. Mature trees, older architecture with real character, and prices to match. Highly sought after and rarely available.
  • Holmes-Foster — south and west of downtown, tree-lined, walkable, a mix of restored early-20th-century homes and mid-century houses. A strong neighborhood identity and an active neighborhood association.
  • The Highlands — between downtown and campus, the most urban feel in the county. A mix of owner-occupied homes and rentals; check the block carefully.
  • Toftrees — north of town off Waddle Road: golf, newer construction, townhomes and condos. Popular with buyers who want low maintenance and easy access to I-99.
  • Park Forest — mid-century single-family homes on generous lots in the northwest. One of the more attainable ways into the State College Area School District.
  • Gray’s Woods — newer development in Patton Township toward Port Matilda. Larger contemporary homes, its own elementary school, quick I-99 access.

Beyond these, look at Ferguson Township toward Pine Grove Mills, or east toward Boalsburg, where the same budget often buys more house or more land.

What about the State College Area School District?

The State College Area School District serves the borough and a wide surrounding footprint including parts of College, Ferguson, Harris and Patton Townships, feeding into State College Area High School. It’s a significant driver of buyer demand across the whole area.

One practical caution: attendance boundaries are set by the district, they do change, and listing data can lag behind. If a specific school matters to you, verify that address with the district directly before you write an offer. We’ll always point you to the official source rather than guess.

Why buy or sell in State College?

Buyers: the university anchors demand in a way few small markets can match, and the neighborhoods hold their character well. Come pre-approved and be ready to move quickly on the good ones. See the buying process.

Sellers: presentation and pricing decide the outcome here more than anything else, and seasonal timing genuinely matters — the calendar of an academic town is real. See the selling process or get your home’s value.
